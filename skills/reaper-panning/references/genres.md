# Panning by genre

> **In this skill:** this document owns where each part sits from left to right and how wide each genre, and each section of a song, is. Depth (near and far), reverb and delay follow [reverb.md](../../reaper-audio-engineer/references/topics/reverb.md), whose section 10 has a reverb table by genre; FX chains by genre follow [compression.md](../../reaper-audio-engineer/references/topics/compression.md), Part 7; levels and loudness follow [gain-staging.md](../../reaper-audio-engineer/references/topics/gain-staging.md).

These are the familiar conventions of each genre, not rules. Frequencies are starting areas; adjust them to the song. Every row assumes the [rules for every genre](../SKILL.md#rules-for-every-genre): kick, bass, snare and lead vocal in the centre, mono below about 100–120 Hz, width from doubles, stereo instruments and effects rather than from panning everything to the edges.

Columns:

- **Centre**: what owns the middle and the low end, in order of importance.
- **Sides**: what sits off centre, and how far.
- **Width through the song**: how the stereo image changes between sections. A dash means the genre keeps one image.

## Pop and vocal-led

| # | Genre | Centre | Sides | Width through the song |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Modern pop | Lead vocal, the loudest and closest part; a tight kick and bass; snare or clap just under the vocal | Doubled backing vocals hard left and right; stereo synths and guitars | Verse narrow, chorus clearly wider |
| 2 | Ballad | Lead vocal; a small, round bass; soft drums that often enter late | A wide stereo piano as the bed; strings spread across the back; acoustic guitar and harmonies off to either side | Grows section by section: the last chorus is the widest and densest |
| 3 | K-pop | Several singers matched in tone; a hip-hop or EDM low end; rap parts drier and closer than sung parts | Very dense vocal stacks; ad-libs panned to both sides | Verse narrow, pre-chorus opening, chorus as wide as it gets. Each section is almost a different arrangement |
| 4 | R&B / neo-soul | A warm, round, loud bass; a softer kick than hip-hop; a dry snare or rim; an intimate vocal | Dense stacked harmonies panned wide, playing the role of a pad; a stereo Rhodes (tremolo, chorus); guitar licks on one side | — |
| 5 | Vietnamese bolero and lyrical songs | A very loud, forward vocal; an even, round bass; light percussion (bongo, shaker, rim) keeping time | Rhythm guitar on one side; sax, violin, mandolin or accordion off centre, filling the gaps between sung lines: the melody instruments answer the vocal instead of playing over it | — |

## Hip-hop and beat-driven

| # | Genre | Centre | Sides | Width through the song |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 6 | Hip-hop / trap | The 808, both the bass and the main character; a short kick layered on it; a dry, close vocal | Ad-libs panned to both sides; bright hi-hats with rolls, sometimes auto-panned | — (few parts and a lot of empty space) |
| 7 | Boom bap | A punchy kick and snare; a warm mono bass; a dry vocal level with the drums | Narrow: jazz or soul samples close to mono; scratches and cuts off to one side | Narrow throughout |
| 8 | Lo-fi / chill | Keys or guitar lead, slightly off centre; a round kick; a small, soft snare; no lead vocal, only chops | Narrow to medium; vinyl crackle and ambience spread wide as a bed | — |
| 9 | Afrobeats | A soft, round kick; a melodic bass or log drum, loud; a warm vocal | Layered percussion (shaker, conga, rim) panned around the image to create movement; call-and-response vocals on both sides; guitar licks off to one side | — |
| 10 | Reggaeton / Latin urban | The dembow (4/4 kick plus an offbeat snare) as the backbone; sub bass following the kick; a close vocal; a dry, sharp snare almost as loud as the vocal | Harmonies and ad-libs panned; Latin percussion (bongo, timbal, güira) panned slightly | — |

## Electronic

| # | Genre | Centre | Sides | Width through the song |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 11 | Big-room EDM / future bass | The kick, the loudest part; the bass, sidechained until it audibly pumps; the lead | Supersaws and chord stacks fill both walls, high-passed to leave the sub to the centre | Breakdown wide and deep; the drop closer and drier |
| 12 | House / tech house | The 4/4 kick and the bassline, locked together and most of the track; clap on beats 2 and 4 | Open hats on the offbeat; percussion and shakers panned slightly; chord stabs and pads in stereo. Deep house: wide pads; tech house: dry and tight | — |
| 13 | Techno | The kick and its "rumble" (a low-passed, sidechained reverb of the kick) filling the sub and low end; hats and ride near the centre | The low end almost entirely mono; width only from effects and delay | — (change comes from filter automation, not from the image) |
| 14 | Drum & bass | The snare, the anchor; a short kick; a mono sine sub below about 100 Hz | A stereo mid-bass layer (reese), distorted and moving, kept separate above the sub; wide hats and shakers | Liquid: pads far and wide. Neuro: almost dry |
| 15 | Dubstep / bass music | A huge half-time kick and snare; a separate mono sub; the mid-bass (growl, wobble) in the lead-vocal role | The mid-bass in stereo only from about 150–200 Hz up; wide effects and pads in the intro | Intro and breaks very wide; the drop almost dry. The wide–narrow, wet–dry contrast carries the drama |
| 16 | Synthwave | A bass synth playing 1/8 notes or arpeggios, sidechained to the kick; the lead synth; an 80s snare | Wide analog pads with chorus; electronic toms panned across the image | — |
| 17 | Ambient | No clear foreground; a soft drone as the low end, with no punch | Pads, textures and field recordings fill the whole image | Slow movement with pan and filter automation |

## Rock and band

| # | Genre | Centre | Sides | Width through the song |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 18 | Rock | A loud snare; the bass locked with the kick; the vocal level with the band, not on top of it as in pop; the guitar solo, as loud as the vocal | Rhythm guitar played twice, hard left and right; toms panned to match the kit; wide overheads | — |
| 19 | Punk / pop-punk | A bright, loud snare; a gritty bass whose notes are heard; the vocal louder than in rock, often doubled | Midrange-heavy guitars left and right; harmonies on both sides | — |
| 20 | Metal | Kick and snare, often reinforced with samples to stay even at speed; the vocal set inside the band; the bass under the guitars, split into a clean low band and a distorted mid band | Four guitar layers, two left and two right, high-passed around 80–100 Hz so the low end stays with the bass and kick; toms panned wide for fills | — |
| 21 | Indie / alternative | The vocal level with the band or slightly behind it; a melodic bass, clearly heard | Often asymmetric: one guitar off to the left, keys off to the right | — |
| 22 | Shoegaze / dream pop | A quiet, whispered vocal sunk into the band like an instrument; the bass, which holds the song's shape more than expected | Layered walls of fuzz, chorus and reverb guitars filling the whole image | — (everything wet: depth follows reverb.md) |

| # | Genre | Centre | Sides | Width through the song |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 23 | Funk | A dry, close, very tight kick, bass and snare; a loud bass with enough midrange to hear the notes and the slap | A thin, bright 16th-note guitar on one side, clav or keys answering on the other; the horns moving as one block | — |
| 24 | Disco | A 4/4 kick; an octave bass; the vocal and harmonies | Bright offbeat open hats; rhythm guitar off to one side; piano or Rhodes; strings high and wide | Glossier and wider than funk |

## Acoustic, traditional and orchestral

| # | Genre | Centre | Sides | Width through the song |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 26 | Acoustic / folk | A close, natural vocal; a small bass and percussion, only supporting | Acoustic guitar recorded in stereo, or doubled left and right; harmonies off to one side. Few tracks, so each one is large and detailed | — |
| 27 | Country | The vocal, the loudest of the band genres; clean, solid drums and bass behind it | Doubled strummed acoustic guitars left and right; pedal steel, fiddle, Telecaster and banjo each at its own pan position, taking turns to fill between sung lines | — |
| 28 | Blues | The lead guitar as a second voice answering the vocal; a round bass | Organ, harmonica or piano off to one side | — |
| 29 | Jazz combo | The horn or the vocal; the double bass. The kick is small and not the centre of attention | A recreated stage: commonly piano left, drums right | — |
| 30 | Orchestral / film | Woodwinds, with brass and percussion behind them | Pan by seating: first violins on the left, moving across to cellos and double basses on the right | — (depth comes from real microphone distances in one hall: reverb.md, section 10) |

Hybrid film scores add big drums and a synth sub to an orchestral layout: both belong in the centre and in mono, like any other low end.

## Width patterns at a glance

The 30 genres use five ways to make width. Name the pattern before placing parts; it decides which parts are paired, which stay alone off centre, and which move.

| Pattern | How the sides are filled | Genres |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Symmetric doubles | Two performances of one part, hard left and right | Pop backing vocals, rock and punk guitars, metal (four layers), country and folk acoustics, K-pop stacks |
| Answering pairs | Two different parts on opposite sides, one answering the other | Funk (guitar and clav), reggae (skank and organ), indie (guitar and keys), Afrobeats (call and response) |
| One side only | A single part off centre, with nothing paired against it | Boom bap scratches, R&B and Afrobeats guitar licks, blues organ or harmonica, bolero rhythm guitar, lo-fi lead slightly off centre |
| A recreated stage | Positions where the players sit | Jazz combo, orchestral, and the drum kit in any band genre |
| Width from effects | Dry parts near the centre; the width lives in reverb, delay and stereo synths | Techno, house, EDM supersaws, ambient, shoegaze |

Sectional contrast (narrow verse, wide chorus or drop) is a separate choice that any pattern can use: pop, ballad, K-pop, EDM and dubstep depend on it.

A single part on one side with no partner leaves that side heavier. Check the balance of the two channels after placing it: [Checking a pan decision](../SKILL.md#checking-a-pan-decision).
